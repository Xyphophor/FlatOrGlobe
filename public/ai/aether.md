*Aether · history, and the arguments built on it*

Flat-earth and geocentric videos often cite the same handful of nineteenth-century experiments: “Airy's failure”, Michelson–Morley, Sagnac, Michelson–Gale, Dayton Miller, and the claim that Lorentz and Einstein invented relativity to hide a motionless Earth. This tab tells the story in order, simply: what the aether was supposed to be, what each experiment measured, which ideas it ruled out, and how special relativity came out of it. Two things are worth knowing before starting. None of these experiments looks at the *shape* of the Earth: they are about motion, and give the same answer on a ball or on a disc. And the one kind of motion that light can detect without looking outside, rotation, has been found every time a loop of light was built to look for it.

## Light is a wave. A wave in what?

**Sound is a wave in air, ripples a wave in water. So what does light wave in?** Huygens described light in 1690 as a disturbance spreading through a very fine matter that fills space[1]. Young made light interfere with itself in 1801–1804, bright and dark bands where two waves add or cancel, which particles cannot do[2]. Fresnel showed in the 1810s that the waves are transverse, shaking sideways like a rope, not back and forth like sound. A wave needs something to wave: the *luminiferous aether*, the “light-carrying air”, invisible, weightless, filling even the vacuum between the stars, and at rest.

$${c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\,\varepsilon_0}}}$$

**Maxwell's surprise.** μ₀ and ε₀ say how strongly currents attract and how strongly charges attract; both come from bench experiments with coils and charged plates, nothing to do with light. In 1862 Maxwell found that waves of electric and magnetic field must travel at 1/√(μ₀ε₀), worked it out from Weber and Kohlrausch's electrical measurements, 310,740 km/s, and set it beside Fizeau's measured speed of light, 314,858 km/s[4]. Light is an electromagnetic wave. With today's constants the formula gives 299,792 km/s. The aether became the medium of electric and magnetic fields too, and the speed c in Maxwell's equations seemed to be the speed *relative to the aether*. If the Earth moves through it, light should go faster one way than the other. Finding that difference became the goal of the century.

## 1727: the Earth's speed seen in the stars

**Bradley was looking for parallax and found something else.** In 1725–1728 he watched γ Draconis pass overhead near London. It swung north and south by about 20 seconds of arc over the year, but three months out of step with what parallax would give, and by the same angle as every other star in the same part of the sky, near or far. He found the cause on a boat: a vane on the mast changes direction when the boat turns, though the wind has not changed[5]. The telescope must be tipped forward into the light, as a walker tips an umbrella forward into vertical rain. The full measurement, with today's data, is in the [Orbit](/ai/orb.md) tab.

*Figure caption:* **Starlight on a moving telescope.** The light falls straight down at c while both telescopes move to the right at v. In the one pointed straight at the star, the back wall catches up with the light before it reaches the eye. The one tipped forward by α, with tan α = v/c, lets it through. The Earth's speed is drawn 3,500 times too large, so the angle is 19° instead of 20.5″. Click to pause.

$${\tan\alpha = \frac{v}{c}}$$

v is the Earth's speed across the line to the star, c the speed of light. With v = 29.78 km/s, α = 20.5″. The aether story starts easily here: a still aether, an Earth moving through it, light arriving in straight lines, and the telescope tipped by the ratio of the two speeds. The angle is the same for all stars in a given direction because it depends on *our* velocity, not on theirs.

## 1810–1851: does moving glass or water carry light along?

**Arago expected starlight to bend differently through a prism** when the Earth moved toward a star than away from it. In 1810 he found no difference at all[6]. Fresnel answered in 1818 with a precise guess: the aether stays still, but a transparent body carries along a fraction of it, just enough to hide the motion: the fraction 1 − 1/n², where n is its refractive index[3]. Nothing for air (n ≈ 1), 0.44 for water. In 1851 Fizeau sent two beams of light through water flowing in opposite directions and compared them: the light was dragged, and by Fresnel's fraction, not by the full speed of the water[7]. Michelson and Morley repeated it in 1886 and confirmed it[31].

$${u = \frac{c}{n} + v\left(1 - \frac{1}{n^2}\right)}$$

u is the speed of light in the lab, c/n its speed in still water, v the water's speed. f = 1 − 1/n² = 0.437 for water. In Fizeau's tubes the water ran at about 7.06 m/s: light gained 3.09 m/s, not the full 7.06 m/s. At the time this was the strongest support for a still aether with a moving Earth. Fifty years later, relativity gave the same formula without any aether (below).

## 1871: “Airy's failure”, a telescope full of water

**The idea is Boscovich's, from 1766.** Light is slower in water. If the telescope moves and the light inside crawls along at c/n, it should take longer to cross the tube, and the tube should need more tilt: fill the telescope with water and the aberration should grow. Klinkerfues claimed in 1867 that it did. Airy built a zenith telescope with a tube of water at Greenwich and watched γ Draconis, Bradley's star, in the spring and autumn of 1871: the aberration was exactly the same as with air[8].

> **What the videos say.** “Airy proved that the telescope is not moving. If the Earth moved, water would have changed the aberration. Since it did not, it is the stars that move around a motionless Earth: *Airy's failure*.”

$$\alpha_{\text{in}} = \frac{\alpha}{n}\ \text{(Snell)}\qquad {\frac{v - f v}{c/n} = \frac{v}{n c} = \frac{\alpha}{n}}\ \text{(Fresnel)}$$

**Fresnel had predicted Airy's result 53 years before it was measured**[3][31]. Two angles inside the tube must agree. The first is where the light goes: entering the water at the angle α = v/c, it bends toward the axis to α/n (Snell's law). The second is where it must go to reach the eyepiece: the tube moves at v, the light is dragged at f v, so it drifts back relative to the tube at v − f v while crossing at c/n. With f = 1 − 1/n², both angles are α/n, 15.4″ for n = 1.333. The water changes nothing. Without Fresnel's drag the ray and the tube would disagree by 11.9″, which Airy would have seen. So Airy's null result is not a failure of the moving Earth: it is the predicted result of a moving Earth and Fresnel's drag, the same drag Fizeau had measured in running water twenty years before.

**Could the stars be the ones moving?** Kinematically, aberration only shows the *relative* velocity between the telescope and the light, and relativity agrees that a motionless Earth with everything else swinging around it is a possible description (the last section). But in a still aether, a star's own motion does not tip its light: the star is simply seen where it was when the light left it. Aberration is the same 20.5″ for every star, near or far, and every star swings in step with the Sun's year. The stars would have to move each on its own ellipse, sized to its own distance so that all the angles come out equal, all keeping time with the Sun. A moving Earth gives it with one number. The [Orbit](/ai/orb.md) tab adds the stars' Doppler shift, which swings by ±30 km/s with the same year. And none of this involves the shape of the ground: a flat Earth in a still aether would face Airy's question exactly as a round one.

## 1881–1887: Michelson and Morley look for the aether wind

**All the earlier experiments measured effects of order v/c, one part in ten thousand, and Fresnel's drag hid them all.** Maxwell pointed out that a round trip of light would still feel the motion, at order (v/c)², one part in a hundred million, far too small to measure, he thought[31]. Michelson built an instrument that could: split a beam in two, send the halves along two perpendicular arms to mirrors and back, and recombine them. Any difference in the two travel times moves the interference fringes. A swimmer explains why. Crossing a river and back takes less time than swimming the same distance upstream and back down, because what the current takes going up it does not fully give back coming down. If the Earth moves through the aether, the arm along the motion is the upstream swim.

*Figure caption:* **The race Michelson set up.** Left: the instrument as an aether supporter expected it, with the aether blowing past along one arm. Both pulses leave the half-silvered mirror together; the one along the wind goes up at c − v and back at c + v, the one across goes at √(c² − v²) both ways, and it comes back first. Right: what was measured, the two pulses back together, as if there were no wind. The wind is drawn at half the speed of light, 5,000 times the Earth's orbital speed, so that the delay can be seen. Click to pause.

$$t_{\text{∥}} = \frac{2L/c}{1 - v^2/c^2},\qquad t_{\text{⊥}} = \frac{2L/c}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}},\qquad {\Delta t \approx \frac{L}{c}\,\frac{v^2}{c^2}}$$

L is the length of each arm, v the speed of the aether wind. In 1887, mirrors folded the light path to L = 11 m; with v = 30 km/s, v²/c² = 0.99 × 10⁻⁸ and Δt = 0.36 × 10⁻¹⁵ s. Turning the instrument by 90° swaps the arms, so the fringes should move by 2(L/λ)(v/c)² = 0.39 of a fringe. Michelson and Morley saw less than a fortieth of that[10]. Their first attempt, at Potsdam in 1881, was already null, but with arms of 1.2 m it was too small to be sure[9]. They also measured at different times of day and planned to repeat it at different seasons: even if the Earth happened to stand still in the aether one day, six months later it would move at 60 km/s relative to that day.

> **What the videos say.** “Michelson–Morley found no motion of the Earth. The simplest explanation is that the Earth does not move. Scientists would not accept it, so they invented relativity.”

**What the experiment actually tested is motion through the aether, not motion around the Sun.** A motionless Earth would indeed give a null result, if the aether were also motionless around it. But then aberration, which needs the light and the Earth to move relative to each other by 30 km/s, swinging through the year, would have no cause; and if the aether swept past a fixed Earth at that speed to make aberration, Michelson would have measured the wind. A motionless Earth explains one of the two results, not both. The second point is that the interferometer cannot tell the shape of anything: the same arms on a disc give the same null result. And the third: rotation *is* visible to a loop of light, at Michelson's own hands in 1925 (below). A bench instrument with two straight arms, like his of 1887, encloses no area and is blind to it.

## 1889–1904: saving the aether, contraction and “local time”

**Physicists did not give the aether up after 1887; they tried to keep it.** Stokes had proposed in 1845 that the Earth drags the aether completely along at its surface, which would explain a null result, but Lorentz showed in 1886 that no smooth flow could both stick to the Earth and give the aberration[11]. FitzGerald in 1889, then Lorentz in 1892, suggested something stranger: a body moving through the aether shrinks along its motion, by exactly the amount that cancels Michelson's time difference[12]. Lorentz grounded it on the idea that the forces holding matter together are electromagnetic, and so are affected by motion through the aether as light is.

$${L_{\text{∥}} = L\,\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad t_{\text{∥}} = \frac{2L/c}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}} = t_{\text{⊥}}$$

Shorten the arm along the motion by √(1 − v²/c²) and the two round trips take the same time at any speed: no fringe moves. For the Earth's 30 km/s, the shortening is 5 parts in a billion, 0.05 µm on Michelson's 11 m path. No ruler could see it, because the ruler shrinks too.

**Contraction alone was not enough.** Lorentz added in 1895 a “local time” for moving bodies, a mathematical device at first, and in 1904 wrote the full set of transformations between a frame at rest in the aether and a moving one; Larmor had found the slowing of moving clocks in 1897–1900[31]. Poincaré named them after Lorentz in 1905 and showed that they form a group, and that the principle of relativity holds exactly with them[13]. Lorentz kept his aether to the end of his life. His theory was the aether, at rest, but with every effect of moving through it exactly hidden.

> **What the videos say.** “Lorentz contraction is an *ad hoc* invention. It was made up to rescue the moving Earth from Michelson–Morley, and has never been seen.”

**It was ad hoc in 1889, and the honest account says so.** What changed is that it was then tested by experiments it was not designed for. Kennedy and Thorndike (1932) built an interferometer with arms of *unequal* length: contraction alone predicts a fringe shift that changes over the day and year, and none was seen; only contraction together with slowed clocks gives zero[16]. Ives and Stilwell (1938) measured the slowed clocks directly, as a shift in the light of fast-moving hydrogen ions[16]. The muons of the next section live 29 times longer than at rest, and a particle collider is built on these formulas. An idea that began as a patch became a predicted, measured effect. Nor did any of it have anything to do with the Earth's shape: the whole debate was about a flat bench with two arms on it.

## 1905: Einstein drops the aether

**Einstein started from two rules instead of a medium.** First, the principle of relativity: the laws of physics are the same in every frame moving uniformly, so no experiment inside a smoothly moving cabin can tell its speed; Galileo had said it for ships in 1632 ([Basics](/ai/phys.md)). Second, light in vacuum always goes at c, whatever the speed of its source. Together they seem to contradict each other, until one accepts that time and length depend on the frame. Lorentz's transformations then follow from the two rules, with no aether behind them, and Einstein wrote that the introduction of a “light aether” would prove superfluous[14]. The aether was not disproved by one experiment: it became undetectable by construction, and then unnecessary.

*Figure caption:* **Two identical light clocks.** Each tick is a round trip of light between two mirrors. The clock on the right passes at 0.6 c: its light, at the same speed c, runs along a longer zigzag, so each tick takes γ = 1.25 times longer. After ten ticks of the clock at rest, the moving one has ticked eight times. Click to pause.

$${\Delta t = \gamma\,\Delta\tau},\qquad \gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}},\qquad x^{\prime} = \gamma\,(x - v t),\quad t^{\prime} = \gamma\left(t - \frac{v x}{c^2}\right)$$

**The light clock.** A pulse bounces between two mirrors; each tick is a round trip. Seen from a frame where the clock moves at v, the pulse runs along a longer zigzag at the same speed c, so each tick lasts longer by γ: moving clocks run slow. τ is the time the clock itself shows. For the Earth's orbital speed γ − 1 = 4.9 × 10⁻⁹. Muons made by cosmic rays live 2.2 µs at rest; in CERN's storage ring at γ = 29.33 they lived 64.4 µs, against 64.4 µs predicted[17]. Atomic clocks flown around the world in 1971 gained and lost what was predicted[18].

$${u = \frac{u^{\prime} + v}{1 + u^{\prime} v / c^2}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad u^{\prime} = \frac{c}{n}:\ \ u \approx \frac{c}{n} + v\left(1 - \frac{1}{n^2}\right)$$

**Velocities no longer simply add.** u′ is a speed measured in a moving frame, v the frame's speed, u the result. At everyday speeds the denominator is 1 and u = u′ + v; near c the sum never passes c. Put in light in water, u′ = c/n, and the formula gives Fresnel's drag, coefficient and all, as von Laue showed in 1907[15]. Fizeau's and Airy's results, once the best evidence *for* an aether, are consequences of relativity with no aether at all.

$$\frac{\Delta f}{f} \approx -\frac{v^2}{2c^2} + \frac{GM}{c^2}\left(\frac{1}{R} - \frac{1}{r}\right)$$

**The same formulas in every phone.** A GPS satellite moves at 3.87 km/s at r = 26,560 km from the Earth's centre. Its speed slows its clock by 7.2 µs a day (special relativity); the weaker gravity up there speeds it up by +45.7 µs a day (general relativity). Net: +38.5 µs a day, which at the speed of light is about 11 km of range error a day. The clocks are tuned slow before launch to cancel it[19] ([GPS](/ai/gps.md)).

**Two rows survive everything, and the table says so honestly.** Lorentz's aether theory predicts exactly what special relativity predicts; it keeps a frame of absolute rest that no experiment can ever find. Physics chose Einstein's version because it says the same with fewer assumptions, not because Lorentz's was refuted. The motionless Earth fails on aberration, the first experiment of the list, and needs a rotating aether for the next section. None of the rows says anything about whether the ground is curved.

## 1913–1925: rotation, which light does see

**Uniform motion cannot be felt from inside; rotation can.** A merry-go-round pushes you outward with your eyes closed. Light feels it too. Send two beams round a closed loop in opposite directions: if the loop turns, the beam going with the turn has further to go before reaching the starting point, which has moved on, and arrives later. Sagnac did it on a turntable in 1913. He was an aether supporter, and titled his paper “the luminous aether demonstrated”[23]. But von Laue had already worked out the same effect from special relativity in 1911, two years before the experiment[24]: the result needs rotation relative to the local non-rotating frames, not an aether.

*Figure caption:* **Two pulses round a loop.** Both leave the splitter (black dot) together, one each way, at the speed of light. Left, the loop is at rest: they meet it again at the same moment. Right, the loop turns: the splitter moves on while they travel, the pulse going against the turn meets it early and the one going with the turn late, at 2πR/(c ∓ ωR). The rim is drawn turning at a quarter of the speed of light; the Earth's surface turns at most at 465 m/s. Click to pause.

$${\Delta t = \frac{4 A\,\Omega}{c^2}},\qquad \Delta N = \frac{4 A\,\Omega \sin\varphi}{\lambda\,c}$$

A is the area enclosed by the loop, Ω its rate of turn, λ the wavelength, φ the latitude: a loop lying flat on the ground feels only the part Ω sin φ of the Earth's rotation. In 1925, Michelson, Gale and Pearson laid pipes in a rectangle of 2010 × 1113 feet (20.8 hectares) at Clearing, Illinois, pumped the air out and sent light both ways round. With the Earth's sidereal rotation the formula gives 0.236 fringe; they measured 0.230 ± 0.005[25]. Today ring lasers a few metres across read the Earth's rotation continuously, down to small wobbles of its axis[26] ([Rotation](/ai/rot.md)).

> **What the videos say.** “Sagnac proved the aether exists. Michelson–Gale measured the aether turning around a motionless Earth. Michelson–Morley shows the Earth does not move, Michelson–Gale shows the sky does.”

**Sagnac's own reading lost, but his measurement stands.** Relativity predicts the same Δt = 4AΩ/c² without a medium, so the effect does not choose the aether. What Michelson–Gale measured is a rotation of Ω sin φ with Ω the sidereal rate: one turn in 23 h 56 min, relative to the stars, not to the Sun. The geocentric reading needs a universe, aether and stars included, turning around the Earth every 23 h 56 min, and dragging with it every other effect of rotation: the Foucault pendulum's turn ([Foucault](/ai/fou.md)), the gyroscopes of aircraft ([Aviation](/ai/avia.md)), the equatorial bulge and the weaker gravity at the equator ([Gravity](/ai/grav.md)), each with the right value at each latitude. One turning Earth gives all of them. And the two Michelson experiments do not contradict each other: the 1887 instrument had straight arms enclosing no area, blind to rotation by design and to uniform motion by relativity. **What would have changed the verdict:** a loop at rest on the ground reading zero. Every such loop since 1925 has read Ω sin φ.

## 1921–1933: Dayton Miller's aether wind, and what came after

**One physicist kept finding a wind.** Dayton Miller, who had worked with Morley, repeated the experiment for years, on Mount Wilson in the 1920s, and reported a small periodic fringe shift corresponding to a wind of about 9 to 10 km/s[27]. It is the result most often quoted against relativity. In 1955 Shankland and colleagues went through Miller's original sheets: the signal followed the temperature of the hut, warmed unevenly by the sun, and not the stars[28]; a 2006 reanalysis found that the instrument's own drift was larger than the signal[28]. Meanwhile every other repetition came out null, with better instruments: Joos in 1930 at Jena saw at most 0.002 fringe where 0.75 was expected[20].

*Figure caption:* How tightly the aether wind has been ruled out, as the largest difference allowed between the light's round-trip times along two directions, as a fraction of the time. The dashed line is what a 30 km/s wind would give. Interferometers 1881–1930[9][10][20]; rotating optical cavities 2009 and 2015[21][22]. Logarithmic scale: each step is a factor of ten.

**Today's versions are a hundred million times more sensitive than 1887.** Lasers locked to optical cavities of sapphire or glass, turned on a table, compare the light's speed along two directions to one part in 10¹⁸, and find no difference[21][22]. A Miller-sized wind would be ten billion times larger than the limit.

## “Either coordinate system with equal justification”: what relativity says about the Earth

> **What the videos say.** “Einstein himself admitted that you cannot tell whether the Earth moves around the Sun or the Sun around the Earth. So the heliocentric model is a choice, not a fact, and the globe with it.”

**The quotation is real.** Einstein and Infeld wrote in 1938, introducing general relativity, that if the laws of physics hold in every coordinate system, “the struggle, so violent in the early days of science, between the views of Ptolemy and Copernicus would then be quite meaningless. Either CS could be used with equal justification”[29]. It means one may *compute* in a frame where the Earth stands still. In that frame, though, the equations contain centrifugal and Coriolis terms, and a far-off universe sweeping round at enormous speeds; the pendulums, the bulge, the gyroscopes, the satellites come out exactly as in the other frame ([Basics](/ai/phys.md), frames of reference). Choosing a frame changes the velocities in the description; it does not change what instruments read.

$${\Delta D = D\left(1 - \sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}\right) \approx D\,\frac{v^2}{2c^2}}$$

**A change of frame does not change a shape.** The distance between two points on the ground, measured with instruments resting on the ground, is the same whatever frame one uses to describe the Earth's motion. The one thing relativity changes is the Earth's length along its motion as described from the Sun's frame: D = 12,742 km and v = 30 km/s give 6.3 cm, five billionths of the diameter, invisible on any drawing and the same for a disc. No choice of coordinates makes a ball flat or a disc round; the shape is measured by the other tabs ([Results](/ai/results.md)).

## The recurring arguments, one by one

**Each argument, what the experiment measured, and what it says about the Earth's motion and its shape.**

| The argument | What was measured | Motion | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| “Airy's failure”: water did not change aberration, so the stars move | The same 20.5″ with water, as Fresnel predicted for a moving Earth in 1818[3][8] | fits the moving Earth; the alternative needs each star's own synchronised ellipse | nothing |
| Michelson–Morley found no motion | No difference along two arms, less than 1/40 of 0.4 fringe[10] | no detectable aether; aberration still needs relative motion of 30 km/s | nothing |
| Lorentz contraction is ad hoc | Later: Kennedy–Thorndike, Ives–Stilwell, muons, flying clocks, GPS[16][17][18][19] | the effects are measured, at the predicted size | 6 cm on the Earth's diameter, from the Sun's frame |

| Miller found an aether wind | A signal that followed the hut's temperature[28] | no wind, to 1 part in 10¹⁸ today[22] | nothing |
| Einstein said no frame is preferred | A statement about coordinates[29] | any frame can be used; the instruments read the same | a shape does not depend on the frame |

**In one sentence:** the aether was a reasonable idea, tested honestly for a century; the experiments that found nothing found nothing for any Earth, round or flat; the ones that found something found rotation and orbital motion; and relativity, which came out of them, is checked every day by the clocks that position a phone.
